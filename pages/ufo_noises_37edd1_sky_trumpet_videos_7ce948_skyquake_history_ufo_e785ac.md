## Introduction

Long before YouTube compilations, UFO channels and viral “sky trumpet” recordings, people were already reporting unexplained sounds that seemed to come from the sky. Across North America, Europe and Asia, witnesses described cannon-like [booms]({{ 'booms/' | relative_url }}), distant metallic rumbling, trumpet-like tones and explosive cracks with no obvious source. These events are now often grouped under the term “skyquakes”: mysterious sounds heard overhead or across the landscape that cannot immediately be linked to storms, earthquakes or human activity.

This older history matters because it shows that modern UFO-related sound claims did not appear from nowhere. Viral sky trumpet videos belong to a much longer tradition of reports that stretch back centuries. While some cases have since received partial geological or atmospheric explanations, many remained unexplained at the time, helping create local legends long before internet conspiracy culture existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyquake" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

## Older Reports of Mysterious Booms and Sky Noises

Historical accounts reveal that unexplained aerial sounds were reported in many different regions under different local names.

One of the most famous examples is the phenomenon known as the **Barisal Guns** in what is now Bangladesh. Nineteenth-century residents reported hearing loud booming noises resembling distant artillery. British colonial officials investigated the reports but failed to identify a clear source. The sounds became well known enough to appear in scientific discussions, local [folklore]({{ 'folklore/' | relative_url }}) and later geological debates. Researchers proposed explanations ranging from unusual wave acoustics to geological activity, but no single theory solved every report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal_guns" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Barisal guns</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Barisal guns</span></span></span>

In the United States, similar sounds became known as the **[Seneca Guns]({{ 'seneca-guns/' | relative_url }})** around Seneca Lake in New York. Witnesses described deep cannon-like detonations rolling across the water and surrounding hills. The reports became part of regional folklore during the nineteenth century and were even referenced by writer James Fenimore Cooper. The sounds were often heard without visible storms, explosions or military activity nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyquake" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Connecticut developed its own version, known as the **Moodus Noises**. The phenomenon was so established that the area's Indigenous name was interpreted as meaning “place of bad noises.” Reports existed before European settlement expanded into the region, suggesting that local populations had noticed the sounds for generations. Unlike some skyquake cases, later geological studies found evidence that shallow seismic activity may contribute to at least part of the Moodus mystery.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://newenglandhistoricalsociety.com/the-mysterious-moodus-noises-of-connecticut/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newenglandhistoricalsociety.com">[New England Historical Society]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newenglandhistoricalsociety.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Today, we know what causes them</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New England Historical SocietyThe Mysterious Moodus Noises of ConnecticutJanuary 11, 2019 — For centuries the strange Moodus noises frigh...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 11, 2019</span></span></span>

Similar traditions appeared elsewhere:

## Why Earlier Witnesses Did Not Connect Them to UFOs

Many modern discussions immediately connect strange [sky sounds]({{ 'sky-sounds/' | relative_url }}) to extraterrestrial explanations. Historical reports show a different pattern.

Before the twentieth century, witnesses generally interpreted mysterious booms through the beliefs available to them. Some communities blamed spirits, divine activity or supernatural forces. Others compared the sounds to hidden battles, distant cannons or geological disturbances. In some regions, the noises became part of local folklore rather than evidence of objects in the sky.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.candidslice.com/legend-of-the-seneca-guns-mysterious-booms-across-east-coast/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: candidslice.com">[Candid Slice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">candidslice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">legend of the seneca guns mysterious booms across east coast</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Candid SliceLegend of the Seneca Guns: Mysterious Booms Across East...30 Oct 2018 — The primary legend believed by European settlers is...</span></span></span>

This difference is important because it demonstrates how cultural context shapes interpretation. The sound itself may remain similar, but the explanation changes with the era. A nineteenth-century listener might think of artillery or spirits. A twenty-first-century listener exposed to UFO videos and social media may think of alien craft, secret technology or apocalyptic events.

The underlying reports therefore reveal a recurring human tendency: unusual sounds are often explained using whatever mysteries dominate the culture at the time.

## How Skyquakes Differ From Viral Trumpet Clips

Although internet videos often present [sky trumpets]({{ 'sky-trumpets/' | relative_url }}) as a unique phenomenon, historical skyquake accounts differ in several important ways.

<img src="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_noises_37edd1_sky_trumpet_videos_7ce948_skyquake_history_ufo_e785ac-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Skyquakes illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_noises_37edd1_sky_trumpet_videos_7ce948_skyquake_history_ufo_e785ac-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/ufo_noises_37edd1_sky_trumpet_videos_7ce948_skyquake_history_ufo_e785ac-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
### Most Historical Reports Described Booms Rather Than Musical Tones

Many older skyquake accounts involve explosive sounds resembling cannon fire, thunder or distant blasting. Witnesses commonly reported a single loud detonation or a sequence of booming sounds.

Modern viral videos often feature sustained metallic groans, horns or resonant “trumpet” noises that last much longer. These recordings create a more dramatic effect and are easier to associate with science-fiction imagery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal_guns" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Barisal guns</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Barisal guns</span></span></span>

### Historical Cases Usually Had Multiple Independent Witnesses

Many famous skyquake traditions developed over decades or centuries. Entire communities reported hearing similar sounds repeatedly, creating local names and folklore around them.

Internet-era clips often rely on a single recording whose origin, editing history or location may be difficult to verify. Once copied and reposted, the same audio can appear under multiple location claims. This makes historical testimony and modern viral evidence fundamentally different kinds of records.

### Earlier Reports Were Not Driven by Online Amplification

A nineteenth-century boom heard near a lake remained a local mystery. Today, a strange sound can become a worldwide story within hours.

This difference affects perception. Historical skyquakes accumulated slowly through newspapers, scientific correspondence and oral accounts. Modern sky trumpet narratives spread through algorithms that reward dramatic interpretations, especially those linked to UFOs or end-times themes.

## The Scientific Search for Explanations

The long history of skyquakes has pushed researchers toward a more cautious conclusion: there may not be one explanation.

The United States Geological Survey notes that reports of mysterious booms have existed for hundreds of years. Some cases eventually prove to be earthquakes, meteor airbursts, sonic booms, industrial activity or atmospheric sound propagation. Others remain unresolved because no measurements were collected when the event occurred.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/earthquake-booms-seneca-guns-and-other-sounds" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: usgs.gov">[usgs.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">usgs.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Earthquake Booms, Seneca Guns, and Other SoundsReports of unidentified “booms” have emerged from different places around the world for hu...</span></span></span>

Several explanations appear repeatedly in scientific discussions:

* Small shallow earthquakes that generate audible booming sounds.
* Meteors or bolides creating atmospheric shock waves.
* Distant thunder carried unusually far by atmospheric conditions.
* Ocean waves and coastal acoustics.
* Gas releases from lake or seabed sediments.

## What History Adds to Modern UFO Sound Claims

The strongest lesson from the pre-internet record is not that every strange sound has been solved. It is that mysterious sky noises are much older than modern UFO culture.

When viral sky trumpet videos appeared online in the 2010s, many viewers treated them as evidence of a new phenomenon. Historical records show otherwise. Reports of unexplained booming, rumbling and horn-like sounds were already being documented in scientific journals, newspapers and local folklore generations before cameras, social media or UFO video channels existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal_guns" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Barisal guns</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Barisal guns</span></span></span><span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal_guns" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Barisal guns</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Barisal guns</span></span></span>
